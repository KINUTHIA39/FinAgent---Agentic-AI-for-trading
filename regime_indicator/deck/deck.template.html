A 1989 model beats the agent panel

Three detectors, one price path, one ground truth. Hamilton's two-state Markov switching model — the benchmark, not the contender — wins on every scope and every scoring rule that matters. The LLM panel does improve on the detector it wraps, significantly. It just wraps the wrong detector.

  • Hamilton flips often — 181 episodes, median 5 days, 2.95 false alarms a year. It is jumpy, and it is right more often than the alternatives.
  • The HMM is stable and stale — 95 episodes, median 21 days. Fewer false alarms, but it arrives late and holds on too long.

Paired tests

Diebold–Mariano on Brier loss with Newey–West standard errors, blocked at the 20-day forward window. Daily observations overlap by 19 of 20 days, so n = 4,781 is roughly n_eff = 239 independent blocks — the tests are run on that basis, not on the raw count.

03

Why the panel helped, and what that was worth

The panel changed almost no labels, yet its Brier score is significantly better than the detector's. That is not a contradiction — it is the whole mechanism. The HMM was pathologically overconfident, and the critic took that away.

Arm B — HMM alone
Arm C — after the panel

Confidence on gated days. The HMM's spike against the right wall is the problem: on 21.6% of these days it claimed above 99% certainty. Brier punishes a confident error far harder than a hedged one.

  • Reliability improved, resolution did not. On gated days reliability fell 0.3015 → 0.1340 while resolution stayed flat at 0.068. The panel did not know more. It stopped pretending to be certain.
  • The critic almost never got its way. It moved to override 38 times; the unanimity gate blocked 34. Only 4 vetoes landed.
  • Zero failed calls across 2,910 model calls, so no abstention was manufactured by an API error.
04

The news layer added nothing

This was the study's central question, and the answer is clean. If sentiment carried information, the panel's advantage would be larger on days that actually have news. It is smaller.

Headline tone does not track index stress

Mean FinBERT and Loughran–McDonald tone by episode. Only COVID shows the expected signature. China 2015 is the most positive period in the sample — more positive than any calm year.

The cause is mechanical: these are single-stock headlines. Through the 2022 drawdown, NVDA coverage is GPU launches and analyst targets, not the selloff. Only COVID was broad enough to dominate every company's news at once.

05

Who got there first

Days between each arm's first bear, stressed call and the hand-dated onset of each episode. Positive is early, negative is late, blank means the arm never reached it.

  • Hamilton reached 7 of 8 onsets against 6 of 8 for the other two, and called the 2022 bear market 38 days ahead — an episode both other arms missed entirely.
  • The panel cost lead time at the GFC. The HMM flagged it on 19 October 2007; the panel's veto pushed the agentic arm to 22 October. Three days later, in the wrong direction.
  • Nobody caught the dot-com peak. It sits in the burn-in window, before scoring opens.
06

Every veto, in full

Four labels changed across the whole study. A veto required all informative specialists to agree against the detector and the critic to concur. Two of the four moved away from bear, stressed immediately before real stress.

07

What this does not show

Constraints that bound the claim, stated rather than buried.

  • News breadth is seven names, and no bank. FNSPID holds ten JPM articles in 23 GB, so the credit specialist works from FRED spreads with no news corroboration.
  • No ticker news before 2011. 102 gated days in 2005–2010 had none, so the sentiment layer was dark across the entire GFC.
  • 2008 never triggered the gate. The HMM sat confidently in bear, stressed throughout, so nothing flipped and the panel was never convened. Arm C is identical to Arm B across the most important episode in the sample.
  • Three states was a taxonomy choice, not a fitted one. BIC falls monotonically from k=2 to k=6 and never turns, so the data does not select three.
  • The drawdown robustness target favours Arm B, which has drawdown as an input feature. Hamilton wins on it anyway, 0.785 to 0.517.

—

Filtered probabilities throughout; smoothed probabilities are forbidden in code, since they condition on the future. Expanding-window standardization, in-sample-only state mapping, and a persistence baseline lagged by the full forward window so it stays feasible at decision time.
